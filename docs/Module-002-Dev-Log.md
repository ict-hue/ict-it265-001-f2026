#### This dev log will be a bit different, as it mainly entails my thoughts regarding energy systems. 
Most of my time, unfortunately, was focused on the IGDA game jam, and I was not particularly focused on the game for this class. That being said:

I have come to a conclusion regarding why opportunity cost is difficult for newer players in games. 

As stated prior, I have been trying to experiment with energy systems in turn based games. However, this delved into a more interesting topic; opportunity cost. As a longtime Pokémon player, opportunity cost is a fundamental aspect of how one plays the game. Swapping Pokémon typically has a negative impact on tempo, and mechanics are in play that opt to harm or strengthen this action (eg. stealth rocks vs. pivot moves). However, tempo also has a strong impact in card games. As someone who has played MTG for years, I also have a decent sense of tempo. However, I cannot justifiably use myself as a metric for research. 

So I gifted a friend Slay the Spire II. They were **bad**.

They would almost always end combats with a net loss of health, but played very defensively on their turns. So what happened?

The main issue was their inability to match the opponent's tempo. The character they played (The Ironclad) is referred to as the "Jock" of the game, and boasts an aggressive playstyle that heals a small amount after each combat. Healing 6 out of his 80 health may seem very minimal, but it had a strong impact on the character's game plan. This was especially evident with the card [**Bash**](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2:Bash).
